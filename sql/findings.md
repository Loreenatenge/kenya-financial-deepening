## Research Question
Has financial deepening in Kenya translated into economic growth,or has the expansion of the financial sector been largely disconnected from real economic activity? Evidence from 1980 to 2024.


## Problem Statement
Kenya's financial sector has grown significantly since the mobile money revolutipn of the lates 2000s, yet private sector credit as a percentage of GDP has remained stubbornly low compared to peer economies,suggesting that financial growth  has not translated into productive credit allocation.


## Finding 1: Financial deepening has increased but slowly
Private credit to GDP rose from 19.68% in the 1980s to 31.58% in the 2020s, a meaningful increase but spread over four decades. Kenya's financial sector has deepened gradually rather than rapidly.

## Finding 2: The relationship between credit and growth is not straightforward
The highest credit years (2014 to 2024) did not consistently produce the highest growth. The 1980s had low private credit but decent GDP growth averaging 4.22%, suggesting early Kenyan growth was driven by agriculture and public investment rather than financial sector development.

## Finding 3: Banking efficiency has improved significantly
The interest rate spread fell from 12.37 percentage points in the 1990s to 4.93 in the 2020s. Kenyan banks were historically extractive, charging borrowers far more than they paid depositors. That gap has narrowed significantly since 2017.

## Finding 4: The mobile money era produced a credit surge then a retreat
Private credit jumped sharply from around 28% in 2013 to 37% in 2015, then declined back to around 31% by 2019. Credit expanded rapidly with financial inclusion but did not sustain that level relative to GDP.

## Finding 5: High credit years show mixed growth outcomes
GDP growth during high credit years ranged from -0.27% in 2020 to 7.59% in 2021, confirming that financial deepening alone does not guarantee economic growth without supportive conditions in other sectors.
